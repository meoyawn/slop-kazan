# Kazan tram system

Trams have a separate OSM topology and directed route membership. `tools/import-kazan-tram-routes.py` reads the same regional PBF as the city importer and checks its SHA-256 before saving the 16 route relations in `tools/data/kazan-tram-routes.json`. `tools/kazan-trams.mjs` compiles those routes against the mapped tram ways, preserving stop order, one-way direction, gauge, electrification, service/access and vertical levels. It reports missing track members or discontinuities instead of drawing a straight connector across a gap.

The current extract contains 186 tram track ways, 212 mapped stop/stop-position records and 59 mapped platforms. Stop-position and platform records may describe the same boarding location; route service uses the relation's ordered stop members. Explicit train-only platforms stay out of the tram system even when another line passes nearby. All 16 directed route memberships are continuous. The mapped 1,524 mm gauge is retained on 183 ways; one way explicitly uses 1,520 mm and two unspecified ways use a labelled 1,524 mm regional estimate. Trams never become heavy-rail links or motor-road links.

The heavy-rail and tram renderers share arc-length sampling, spatial batching, track sections, platforms and overhead geometry through independent path instances. Reserved beds retain ballast and sleepers. Embedded sections keep rails at the road surface, without ballast covering the roadway. The source has no explicit embedded-track tags in the current extract: road-envelope overlap sampled every four metres supplies an explicitly inferred classification, including short at-grade crossing sections. Yamasheva's reserved median and the embedded tracks by the central station are browser review sites. Bridge grades follow mapped structure levels; road-profile blending keeps embedded rails in their own road deck. Shared-node height targets make layer transitions continuous. Tunnel terrain patches merge parallel-track cuts, and exposed entrances stay open. Embedded road sections preserve their pavement rather than carving the rail base into it. Parallel tram tracks and associated road tunnels share an explicitly estimated DEM-based underpass floor; each lane no longer follows a different coarse terrain sample. Structure clearance queries include elevation so an overhead track cannot hide the tram corridor below it. Shared internal walls are omitted and the common roof leaves space for the overhead contact wire. These are inferred sections, not surveyed engineering dimensions.

`js/kazan-trams.js` runs a bounded demonstration fleet on mapped route parts (32 vehicles in lightweight mode, 48 normally). Vehicles accelerate/brake, dwell at mapped stops, stop at estimated tram signals, keep distance from other vehicles and yield to traffic/player obstacles. They use only connected mapped return routes or loops at a terminus. Short public turnback connectors follow the directed track graph; a route with no connected return ends there. Road cars also query the tram body's clearance, including height, so a tram on a bridge does not block traffic below. Route distance, track sampling, vehicle body and overhead contact height use the same profile.

Stop signs and white-T signals are procedural. Signal locations start from mapped tram level crossings; timing uses the existing city signal clock and is a simulation estimate. The red/cream vehicle is a generic tram representation with route lettering and a raised pantograph, not a claim to reproduce a specific fleet unit. This is not a live timetable or dispatch feed. Current service can change independently of OSM; the [municipal transport schedule](https://kazantransport.ru/electric_transport/shedule/) and operator notices should be checked before making a real journey.

Regression coverage checks actual route membership/continuity, stop attachment and distinct reserved/embedded beds. A service regression exercises dwell, a red restriction, obstacle clearance, terminal arrival and separation from traffic on another vertical level. The city data regression fails on the preceding geography, which has no tram system. Heavy-rail topology and tunnel-profile regressions remain in the same suite.
