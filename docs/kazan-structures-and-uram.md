## URAM

The [completed park photographs and project description](https://www.legatoparks.ru/project/uram-park) establish separate concrete bowls, asphalt pump tracks with planted islands, wooden BMX ramps, coloured courts, black floodlight poles and the blue corrugated indoor centre. These references are external; their photographs are not shipped as textures.

`recreation` records retain each OSM activity outline. The first detailed venue uses twelve URAM polygons: the main and children's pump tracks (`1057568991`, `1057568993`), bowls (`1057568992`), air park (`1057568998`), four basketball courts, three futsal courts and the sand court. The same generators take a mapped outline, activity kind and dimensions. They produce bounded static meshes, lane rollers, raised bowl banks, coping, court markings, hoops, goals and a capped set of floodlight models. Exact internal layouts, bank heights and obstacle positions are procedural estimates, not a survey or a riding-physics simulation. Native terrain remains beneath the raised obstacle meshes.

The indoor building is `way/973924084`: mapped footprint, blue corrugated cladding, glazed lower floor, ribbon windows and URAM lettering. Height and hidden elevations remain estimates. The teleport entry identifies the indoor centre and adjoining park. The vegetation pipeline excludes mapped sports polygons throughout the city so trees cannot be seeded onto the playing surfaces. Other venues' detailed equipment can be enabled through the same reviewed activity data.

Activity surfaces use the mesh's ground height and a minimum height above the reservoir datum. Negative DEM samples near the filled shoreline must not bury the bowl floor. Pump-track boundaries use a finer shared mesh in desktop rendering. The small round untyped pavilion in the park follows the same conservative low-rise fallback as other compact unknown outlines; it is not labelled a retail kiosk.

The road renderer now draws pavement as two edge strips. A wide pavement quad beneath the complete carriageway could intersect and cover asphalt on uneven terrain because the surfaces sampled different cross-sections. Ordinary road and ambient-vehicle heights also sample the actual terrain mesh triangles, including refined hill and road patches, instead of an independently interpolated DEM surface. Adaptive subdivision adds road triangles at changes of slope; it does not refine the whole regional terrain. Bridge and tunnel grade profiles remain separate. Terrain vertices are cached and the traffic graph evaluates height only when a nearby obstacle requires it.

The regression on R-239 independently compares the driving surface with its three DEM triangle corners. Before the repair, asphalt was 36 cm below the visible terrain at the checked point. Browser captures cover the same road, both M structures, night lighting, the store, and URAM from above and near the bowl banks. These software-rendered captures are visual validation, not GPU performance measurements.

The reference aerial view also shows the opposite-bank developments, hillside housing and a small red building. Their existing mapped envelopes and material fallbacks remain; their exact facade identities and current finishes need further scene review. Bowls and courts remain separate from motor traffic; this change does not add a road inside an activity outline.
