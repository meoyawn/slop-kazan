# Road levels and distant rendering

The source OSM graph connects roads by node identity. A crossing in plan view does not itself create a junction. Each imported traffic edge and lane connector now carries its source road, including its bridge, tunnel and layer information. Imported carriageways retain their mapped centreline vertices and lane counts rather than using the Manhattan simplification and divided-road heuristics.

Road geometry, markings, vehicle bodies and wheels sample the same road profile. Player movement selects a surface near its current elevation, allowing ground-level movement below an overpass. Motor tunnels are retained in the import. Their open approaches lower the terrain; the covered part has a separate concrete roof and a continuous terrain cut beneath it. This leaves the portal open instead of closing the driving corridor with a grassy slope. Connected approach profiles meet at shared graph nodes, including raised turning ramps and terrain embankments.

Imported short graph links remain traversable even when too short to spawn a vehicle. Dropping them had broken the surrounding circulation and removed both Chistopolskaya overpass directions from the final connected traffic network. Obstacles are checked at the road's own elevation: a column below the deck does not close its upper lanes, while an obstruction at driving height still does.

`js/kazan-road-structures.js` builds shared, spatially batched structural geometry throughout the imported road network: thick bridge slabs, railings, columns and crossheads, retaining walls, tunnel roof slabs, portals and inexpensive emissive tunnel strips. Nearby parallel bridge carriageways with matching name, level, direction and extent share a bounded median slab and outside railings. The filled median also participates in player surface selection. Supports avoid mapped lower road/path corridors, including roads on another bridge level. This is a procedural engineering approximation; exact pier spacing, concrete sections and portal finishes remain unverified.

OSM layers describe ordering rather than engineering elevations. Where an absolute deck or tunnel elevation is unavailable, the renderer uses an explicitly estimated clearance: 6 m per level, transitions along the actual connected approach roads, and the existing end-to-end bowed profile for long river spans. These are approximate structures, not surveyed cross-sections. The underlying FABDEM samples remain unchanged. Regression checks cover Millennium's curved carriageways, both Amirkhan overpasses, Amirkhan tunnel portals and the Pobedy turning ramps.

The original New York borough renderer already keeps inexpensive windows, per-building colours and separate roof colours on distant instanced boxes, with pixel-footprint filtering. Kazan now uses the same approach while carrying its own observed facade palettes and accent zones into those instances. Small window patterns converge to their mean glazing coverage instead of vanishing into solid white walls. Per-instance data shares one interleaved buffer and one material across the city.

The original sky-coloured aerial perspective already integrates decreasing haze density along elevated view rays. Kazan's extra altitude multiplier had weakened it a second time. That multiplier is removed; a fixed 70% of the original preset density preserves some additional visibility across the regional Volga landscape. Lightweight rendering uses a matching altitude-adjusted approximation. The software preview and lightweight renderer also omit G-buffer-only transparent outputs when no G-buffer is enabled.
